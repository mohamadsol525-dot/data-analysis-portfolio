# Project 02 — Sales Performance & Business Insights

## Overview

This project analyzes the sales performance of a food manufacturing company over a three-year period from **1399 to 1401**.

The analysis moves from the overall sales performance to product groups, individual products, sales channels, sales representatives, and customers to identify the main drivers of growth, decline, and concentration risk.

## Business Objective

The main objective is to answer a practical business question:

**Where is sales performance improving, where is it declining, and what parts of the business are responsible for these changes?**

The analysis evaluates performance using multiple complementary indicators rather than relying on revenue alone.

## Analytical Framework

The analysis follows a top-down approach:

**Overall Sales → Product Groups → Products → Sales Channels → Sales Representatives → Customers**

This structure makes it possible to move from identifying a sales anomaly to investigating its underlying sources.

## Key Performance Indicators

Sales performance is evaluated using:

- Sales Quantity
- Sales Weight
- Sales Revenue (IRR)
- Sales Revenue (USD)

Using multiple indicators helps distinguish genuine volume growth from changes that may mainly reflect price or inflation effects.

## Key Findings

### 1. Winter 1401 was the major sales deviation

Winter 1401 was identified as the main departure from the company's historical sales pattern, with declines in sales quantity, weight, and dollar revenue.

### 2. Autumn was the strongest and most consistent season

Autumn was the only season that showed continuous growth across all three years and across all four sales indicators.

### 3. Real sales performance cannot be judged by IRR revenue alone

In several periods, growth in IRR revenue occurred without corresponding growth in physical sales volume or dollar revenue, highlighting the importance of evaluating financial and physical indicators together.

### 4. Non-sesame products were a major source of the 1401 decline

Although sesame products remained the larger product group, non-sesame products contributed disproportionately to the annual decline in sales weight and quantity.

### 5. Sales concentration increased across several dimensions

The analysis identified concentration risk at multiple levels, including products, sales channels, sales representatives, and customers.

### 6. B2B remained the dominant sales channel

B2B was the primary sales channel throughout the period, while the company's dependence on this channel increased over time.

## Product Analysis

The report compares **sesame** and **non-sesame** product groups and then moves deeper into their individual product categories.

Key products and changes in their rankings are examined using dollar sales across annual, seasonal, and monthly periods.

The analysis also identifies situations where one product acts as a growth driver or where concentration in a single product increases business risk.

## Sales Channel Analysis

Sales performance is examined across:

- B2B
- Retail Store
- Online
- Other Channels

The analysis evaluates both the size and stability of each channel and investigates whether changes in overall sales originate from specific channels or reflect broader business patterns.

## Sales Representative & Customer Analysis

The analysis goes beyond identifying the largest sales representatives and customers.

It also evaluates:

- Sales concentration
- Stability over time
- Changes in ranking
- Customer purchasing patterns
- Relationships between customers, sales representatives, and channels

An important finding is that a large customer is not necessarily a stable customer, making customer value more meaningful when size and persistence are considered together.

## Business Insights

The overall analysis suggests that the company's main risk is not simply a decline in total sales, but **concentration across multiple layers of the business**.

High dependence on specific products, channels, sales representatives, and customers means that a change in any one of these areas can have a significant effect on total company performance.

This highlights the importance of diversification, customer retention, channel development, and reducing dependence on individual products and sales relationships.
